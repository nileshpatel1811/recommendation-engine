What Should You Compare?

1. Interest Cost

Interest is one of the most important costs to compare when taking a gold loan. However, borrowers should look beyond a headline rate and confirm the actual rate offered, how interest is calculated, when it is payable, and whether other charges apply.

A lower advertised rate does not automatically mean a lower overall cost for every borrower.

2. Loan Amount and Loan-to-Value

Different lenders may have different eligibility requirements and loan-to-value policies. The amount you can actually borrow depends on factors including the value and characteristics of the gold being pledged and the lender's assessment.

If getting a particular loan amount is your priority, compare eligibility rather than choosing a lender based only on its interest rate.

3. Processing Speed

Processing time can matter when the money is needed urgently. Banks and gold-loan NBFCs can have different processes, branch requirements and turnaround times.

A borrower who needs money immediately may reasonably choose a different lender from someone who has time to compare rates and terms carefully.

4. Interest Payment and Repayment Terms

Before taking a gold loan, understand when interest payments are due and what happens if a payment is delayed. The payment cycle, reminders and other lender policies can differ.

Always ask the lender to explain the consequences of missed or delayed payments before pledging your gold.

5. Switching an Existing Gold Loan

If you already have a gold loan, the best lender for a new loan may not be the best lender for switching that existing loan.

When comparing a switch, consider the existing loan's outstanding amount, current interest cost, applicable charges, transfer process and the terms offered by the new lender.

6. Flexibility

Ask about the flexibility available during the loan. Depending on the lender and product, borrowers may have different options around repayment, releasing pledged gold and other servicing requirements.

Do not assume that a feature available from one lender is available from another. Confirm the current terms directly before taking the loan.

Bank vs. NBFC Gold Loans

Both banks and non-bank financial companies (NBFCs) offer gold loans, but their products and processes can differ.

A bank may be attractive to a borrower who prioritizes interest cost and is comfortable with its process. An NBFC may be more attractive to someone who prioritizes speed or particular servicing features.

There is no universal winner. The better option depends on the borrower's priorities and the terms actually available to them.

Is the Lowest Interest Rate Always Best?

Not necessarily.

Suppose two lenders offer similar loan amounts but one has a lower rate while the other offers faster processing or terms that are more convenient for the borrower. The lower-rate lender may still be the better choice, but the difference in cost should be considered alongside the features that matter to the borrower.

This is why Gold Loan Match asks what matters most before making a recommendation.

What Information Should You Confirm?

Before accepting a gold loan, confirm the current terms directly with the lender, including:

  • Interest rate and how interest is calculated
  • Eligible loan amount
  • Processing and other applicable charges
  • Interest payment schedule
  • Repayment and renewal terms
  • Conditions for releasing pledged gold
  • Consequences of delayed payments
  • Branch and documentation requirements
